## The hero needs to be a real moment, not a title card

Right now it's reading as flat: a headline, a subhead, a button. That's not
enough for the first thing anyone sees. Rebuild it as a rich, layered
composition:

- **Give it a real visual centerpiece**, not just text on a background. Show
  an actual floating mockup of the verdict result — the "This looks like a
  scam" card, tilted slightly, with real depth (soft shadow, layered above
  the background atmosphere) — sitting alongside or behind the headline, so
  a visitor sees the *product itself* in the first three seconds, not just a
  promise of one. This is the single highest-impact addition you can make.
- **Add a second layer of supporting proof directly in the hero**, not just
  below it — a small row of confidence indicators sitting near the CTA:
  something like a checkmark row reading "Free," "No account needed," "Takes
  about 90 seconds," or similar short trust markers. This turns the hero from
  a pitch into a promise with receipts.
- **Push the typography further.** The headline should feel enormous and
  confident — significantly larger than what's there now, using real scale
  contrast between the display serif headline and the smaller supporting
  text, the way an actual magazine cover or a 1Password/Lemonade hero does.
  Don't be shy with the scale.
- **Add a subtle scroll cue** at the bottom of the hero (a small down-arrow
  or "See how it works" link) that visually bridges into the main section
  below, so the page feels like one continuous, considered experience rather
  than a hero that dead-ends.
- **Deepen the atmosphere** — richer, more visible (while still soft and
  legible) gradient movement behind the hero specifically, more than
  anywhere else on the page, since this is the one moment allowed to feel
  the most alive.
