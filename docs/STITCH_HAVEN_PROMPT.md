This is a rename and a full aesthetic pivot. Treat it as a fresh brief, not a
patch on what's there — most of the current palette and mood gets replaced.

## The rename

The product is now called **Haven**, not "Is This Real?". Replace every
instance of the old name — the wordmark, the page title, any header/footer
text — with "Haven". Tagline underneath the wordmark: "Your safe place to
check anything." Keep "Is this real?" only as a question inside body copy
where it reads naturally (e.g. the compose field's placeholder can still ask
"paste the message you're wondering about"), but the brand name itself is
Haven everywhere.

## The new aesthetic: soft dreamcore, not editorial paper

Forget warm cream/paper. The new direction is a **soft, glowing,
pastel dreamscape** — pink and powder-blue skies, clouds, glossy reflective
surfaces catching diffuse light, gentle neon edge-lighting, a liminal but
warm architectural quality. Think: the feeling of walking through a
sunlit, cloud-filled space that's part sky, part building, entirely calm.
Not childish, not neon-loud — soft, premium, dreamlike.

**I'm attaching one real image: a photorealistic 3D render of a glossy
marble interior with a double archway opening onto pastel pink clouds,
traced in soft neon-pink edge lighting.** Use this exact image as the
**hero section's full-bleed background** — do not generate or invent your
own hero image, use the uploaded file. Place the wordmark and headline in
the lower-left third of the frame, over the open reflective floor area
where there's genuine negative space, so the architecture and the clouds
through the arches stay visible above the text. This image is the single
most important asset on the page — build the hero around it, not the other
way around.

**For every other section's atmosphere, do not rely on generated or
uploaded photography — build soft pastel gradient washes directly as
background treatments:** large, extremely diffuse radial/conic gradients
blending blush pink, powder blue, and pale lavender, with no hard edges, no
visible geometric shapes, no recognizable objects — pure soft atmosphere,
like light through frosted glass or distant cloud cover. This should feel
continuous with the hero photo's palette (matching pinks and blues) without
depending on more photographic assets that would need to be generated or
sourced. Vary the intensity — lightest and most neutral directly behind
body text for legibility, warmer and more present behind section headlines
and the verdict result specifically.

## Legibility is non-negotiable

This product is used by people who are frightened and often older —
readability matters more than atmosphere ever gets to. Every gradient wash
must sit clearly behind body text with enough contrast that dark ink-colored
text reads easily on top of it — never place body copy directly over a busy
part of the hero photograph. Use a soft white/cream translucent card or
scrim behind any text block that sits on top of a photographic or gradient
background, so the dreamy atmosphere never fights the words. Verdict cards
in particular need to read instantly and clearly — a "this looks like a
scam" result must never get lost in a pretty background.

## Verdict colors stay grounded

The three verdict states (scam / safe / uncertain) keep their own distinct
colors — a serious red, a reassuring green, a clear amber — as the one place
on the page allowed to depart from the pastel pink/blue palette. Think of it
as: everything is soft and dreamy, and then the answer breaks through like a
patch of clear sky — the one moment of grounded clarity in an otherwise
atmospheric page. Do not let the verdict cards become pastel-pink versions
of themselves; they need to read as clearly different states at a glance,
the way they always have, just now sitting on top of the new atmosphere
instead of warm cream paper.

## What stays the same (the main section was working — keep it)

All the previously-established content structure stays. Restyle it into the
new palette, don't rebuild it from scratch:

1. **Hero** — Haven wordmark, one-sentence explanation, primary "Check a
   message" CTA, built on the uploaded photo as described above.
2. **The problem** — 2-3 sentences on why scam messages are built to survive
   a second look, plus 2-3 large statistics (dollars lost to fraud last
   year, which age group loses the most per victim) with real typographic
   weight.
3. **How it works** — the two-layer explanation: a fast rules pass that
   catches obvious fakes, and a second pass that reads the message the way a
   person would, catching things with zero red flags (the "wrong number"
   opener example). Keep the visual flow/diagram structure already
   established, restyled into the new palette.
4. **See it in action** — an example message alongside its resulting verdict
   card, made concrete and visual.
5. **Trust & privacy** — what's never stored, what is kept instead (a
   one-way scrambled fingerprint), stated plainly and confidently.
6. **The tool itself** — compose → checking → verdict (three states: scam /
   safe / uncertain) → error/rate-limit states, exactly as previously
   specified, restyled into the new look.

## Absolute rule, unchanged from before

Still not a dark security-tool aesthetic — no terminal green, no shield/lock
clichés, no siren-red klaxon styling. The old failure mode was "too flat and
generic." The new direction should never swing into "too loud or gimmicky"
either — soft, premium, and calm, even while being much more visually
ambitious than before.
